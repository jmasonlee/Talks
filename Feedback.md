# Why Feedback Fails (and how it actually works)

## Talk Resources
### Bibliography

| Title | Authors & Citation | Summary | Free Access Link |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Effects of Feedback Interventions on Performance: A Historical Review, a Meta-Analysis, and a Preliminary Feedback Intervention Theory** | Avraham N. Kluger & Angelo DeNisi (1996)<br>*Psychological Bulletin*, 119(2), 254–284. | Introduces Feedback Intervention Theory (FIT), revealing that over a third of feedback interventions decrease performance when evaluations divert cognitive focus away from task execution and toward self-evaluation. | [Link Here](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232458848_The_Effects_of_Feedback_Interventions_on_Performance_A_Historical_Review_a_Meta-Analysis_and_a_Preliminary_Feedback_Intervention_Theory) |
| **Building a Practically Useful Theory of Goal Setting and Task Motivation: A 35-Year Odyssey** | Edwin A. Locke & Gary P. Latham (2002)<br>*American Psychologist*, 57(9), 705–717. | Summarizes 35 years of goal-setting research, demonstrating how specific, challenging targets combined with progress feedback mobilize effort, focus, persistence, and strategy execution on familiar tasks. | [Full PDF (UMN course reading)](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20&%20Latham%20(2002).pdf) |
| **The Effects of Simultaneous Learning and Performance Goals on Performance: An Inductive Exploration** | Aline D. Masuda, Edwin A. Locke, & Kevin J. Williams (2014)<br>*Journal of Cognitive Psychology*, 27(1), 37–52. | Investigates the concurrent pursuit of learning and performance goals, discovering an inverted U-shaped relationship where moderate overall goal difficulty optimizes strategy learning and task performance. | [Link Here](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/280529583_The_effects_of_simultaneous_learning_and_performance_goals_on_performance_An_inductive_exploration) |
| **The Future of Feedback: Motivating Performance Improvement Through Future-Focused Feedback** | Jackie Gnepp, Joshua Klayman, Ian O. Williamson, & Sema Barlas (2020)<br>*PLoS ONE*, 15(6), e0234444. | Shows that backward-looking performance evaluations often trigger defensive attributional disputes, whereas prospective, collaborative problem-solving increases feedback acceptance and intent to improve. | [Open access](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0234444) · [PDF](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0234444&type=printable) |
| **How Positive and Negative Feedback Motivate Goal Pursuit** | Ayelet Fishbach, Tal Eyal, & Stacey R. Finkelstein (2010)<br>*Social and Personality Psychology Compass*, 4(8), 517–530. | Demonstrates that positive feedback motivates when it signals goal commitment, whereas negative feedback motivates when highlighting progress gaps, depending on expertise and goal focus. | [Publisher (paywalled)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2010.00285.x) · [Abstract (BGU)](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/how-positive-and-negative-feedback-motivate-goal-pursuit/) |
| **Social Cognitive Theory of Self-Regulation** | Albert Bandura (1991)<br>*Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 50(2), 248–287. | Details the three subfunctions of self-regulation—self-monitoring, judgment against standards, and self-reactions—showing how proactive goal setting and self-efficacy drive motivated human action. | [Full PDF (Stanford-affiliated SSRL SIG copy)](https://ssrlsig.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/bandura-1991-soc-cog-theory.pdf) · [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-5978(91)90022-L) |
| **Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review** | Nicholas C. Soderstrom & Robert A. Bjork (2015)<br>*Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 10(2), 176–199. | Highlights the crucial distinction between short-term performance during training and long-term retention, showing that conditions that impair immediate performance often foster deeper long-term learning. | [Full PDF (third-party copy)](https://hwb.gov.wales/api/storage/1031b700-acaf-4eda-a4bc-758715e85ff2/Learning%20versus%20Performance%20An%20Integrative%20Review.pdf) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25910388/) |
| **Negative Self-Efficacy and Goal Effects Revisited** | Albert Bandura & Edwin A. Locke (2003)<br>*Journal of Applied Psychology*, 88(1), 87–99. | Defends social cognitive theory against mechanical control-theory models, proving that strong self-efficacy beliefs and high personal standards consistently enhance motivation and performance. | [Full PDF (university-hosted copy)](http://homepages.se.edu/cvonbergen/files/2013/01/Negative-Self-Efficacy-and-Goal-Effects-Revisited.pdf) · [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12675397/) · [Publisher (paywalled)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.88.1.87) |
| **Thanks for the Feedback: The Science and Art of Receiving Feedback Well** | Douglas Stone & Sheila Heen (2014)<br>*Viking / Penguin Group*. | Identifies the three feedback triggers (truth, relationship, identity) that block communication and outlines practical methods to untangle appreciation, coaching, and evaluation. | [goodreads]([https://archive.org/details/thanksforfeedbac0000ston](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/18114120-thanks-for-the-feedback) · Check it out from your local library :-) |
