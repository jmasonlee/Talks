| **The Effects of Simultaneous Learning and Performance Goals on Performance: An Inductive Exploration** | Aline D. Masuda, Edwin A. Locke, & Kevin J. Williams (2014)<br>*Journal of Cognitive Psychology*, 27(1), 37–52. | Investigates the concurrent pursuit of learning and performance goals, discovering an inverted U-shaped relationship where moderate overall goal difficulty optimizes strategy learning and task performance. | [Add Link Here]() |
| **The Future of Feedback: Motivating Performance Improvement Through Future-Focused Feedback** | Jackie Gnepp, Joshua Klayman, Ian O. Williamson, & Sema Barlas (2020)<br>*PLoS ONE*, 15(6), e0234444. | Shows that backward-looking performance evaluations often trigger defensive attributional disputes, whereas prospective, collaborative problem-solving increases feedback acceptance and intent to improve. | [Add Link Here]() |
| **How Positive and Negative Feedback Motivate Goal Pursuit** | Ayelet Fishbach, Tal Eyal, & Stacey R. Finkelstein (2010)<br>*Social and Personality Psychology Compass*, 4(8), 517–530. | Demonstrates that positive feedback motivates when it signals goal commitment, whereas negative feedback motivates when highlighting progress gaps, depending on expertise and goal focus. | [Add Link Here]() |
| **Social Cognitive Theory of Self-Regulation** | Albert Bandura (1991)<br>*Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 50(2), 248–287. | Details the three subfunctions of self-regulation—self-monitoring, judgment against standards, and self-reactions—showing how proactive goal setting and self-efficacy drive motivated human action. | [Add Link Here]() |
| **Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review** | Nicholas C. Soderstrom & Robert A. Bjork (2015)<br>*Perspectives on Psychological Science* / *Oxford Bibliographies*. | Highlights the crucial distinction between short-term performance during training and long-term retention, showing that conditions that impair immediate performance often foster deeper long-term learning. | [Add Link Here]() |
| **Negative Self-Efficacy and Goal Effects Revisited** | Albert Bandura & Edwin A. Locke (2003)<br>*Journal of Applied Psychology*, 88(1), 87–99. | Defends social cognitive theory against mechanical control-theory models, proving that strong self-efficacy beliefs and high personal standards consistently enhance motivation and performance. | [Add Link Here]() |
| **Thanks for the Feedback: The Science and Art of Receiving Feedback Well** | Douglas Stone & Sheila Heen (2014)<br>*Viking / Penguin Group*. | Identifies the three feedback triggers (truth, relationship, identity) that block communication and outlines practical methods to untangle appreciation, coaching, and evaluation. | [Add Link Here]() |
